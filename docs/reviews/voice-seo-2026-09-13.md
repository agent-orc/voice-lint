## Basis and limits

Separate locale URLs let search engines and readers obtain a language version without relying on a preference script. Both versions remain directly reachable; an explicit URL does not redirect based on browser language. [Google: Managing multi-regional and multilingual sites](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites).

The alternate links identify corresponding localized pages, including a default. The test checks reciprocal destinations rather than inferring coverage from the presence of one hreflang tag. [Google: Tell Google about localized versions of your page](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions).

The sitemap supplies canonical content URLs and is advertised from the domain's robots.txt. Discovery is a technical prerequisite; submitting a sitemap does not guarantee indexing. [Google: Build and submit a sitemap](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap).

These checks establish technical crawl readiness for the tested artifact. They do not measure search demand, keyword fit, Google indexing, Search Console coverage, ranking, click-through rate or field Core Web Vitals. No Search Console property was accessed or sitemap submitted. A lab screenshot or lack of horizontal overflow does not establish LCP, INP or CLS performance for real users. Structured-data syntax does not guarantee a search feature.
