AI writing anti-patterns.

Empty prefaces, contrived contrasts and explanations that miss the point. Each rule shows the problem, a possible revision and cases where the text should stay. Its counter-prompt sets out the criteria for that decision.

When AI-style writing obscures the point

A document can contain correct sentences and still miss its task. An introduction explains architecture before the reader can open the app. A heading hedges an established fact. Three paragraphs justify a change instead of describing the current workflow.

The review prompts include context: audience, reader task, supported facts and accepted decisions. An explanation stays when it helps the reader understand a choice, prerequisite or consequence.

Anti-patterns and counter-prompts

Examples make each pattern visible. Open an entry for its reader cost, a possible correction, a counter-prompt and a case where the wording should stay.

Structure and relevance

Taxonomy before the taskOur architecture unifies three review surfaces.

The opening introduces the author’s categories before showing what the reader can do.

Reader cost: Readers must translate the conceptual scheme into their next action.

Pattern example

Our architecture unifies three review surfaces.

Possible revision

Open a folder to review its Markdown files.

The page is a folder-review quickstart and that operation exists.

Can stay as written: This chapter compares three review architectures.

Counter-prompt

Review aim: Lead with the reader's task

Recognition, exceptions and sources

The opening explains the author's framework before making the reader's next action or question clear.

Possible cues: long conceptual preface; implementation taxonomy before first action.

Exceptions: A conceptual essay may aim to establish a framework; a reference page may serve lookup rather than onboarding.

Summary loopsSave your decision. In summary, your choice can be saved. This keeps the decision stored.

The text restates the same conclusion in an introduction, a takeaway and a final summary.

Reader cost: Readers spend attention on repeated information instead of the next useful point.

Pattern example

Save your decision. In summary, your choice can be saved. This keeps the decision stored.

Possible revision

Save your decision.

The repeated sentences add no persistence guarantee or other distinct information.

Can stay as written: Each endpoint lists its request, response and error cases.

Counter-prompt

Review aim: Make repeated structure earn its place

Recognition, exceptions and sources

Sections repeat the same conclusion, stock transition or fixed template without adding a distinct fact or helping comparison.

Possible cues: repeated wrap-up paragraphs; identical feature-benefit cadence; synonymous summaries.

Exceptions: API references, accessibility cues and parallel comparisons benefit from deliberate consistency. Repetition does not establish machine authorship.

  • Use simple words, concise sentences

    Prefer precise verbs and consistent terminology; remove words that add no information. Context and meaning still determine whether a modifier or longer explanation is needed.

Formatting on every sentenceQuality: More than a score. Cost: A question of value.

Nearly every sentence becomes a labeled bullet, a heading or an emphasized fragment.

Reader cost: Visual emphasis stops indicating which relationships and decisions matter.

Pattern example

Quality: More than a score. Cost: A question of value.

Possible revision

Review quality and cost

One section compares quality and cost. The question remains useful as a heading for instructions that answer it.

Can stay as written: How do I compare review models?

Counter-prompt

Review aim: Let structure support reading

Recognition, exceptions and sources

Structure obscures a sequence, comparison or explanation, or an informational heading fails to identify the section's topic or reader task.

Possible cues: one-sentence subsections; nested lists without hierarchy; bold labels on every sentence.

Exceptions: Long references need navigation; procedures need steps; comparison tables can reduce rather than add reading work. A question heading is useful when readers ask that question and the section answers it. An accepted positioning headline can prioritize tone and memorability.

Optional-detail detoursBefore your first review, read about theme choice, display density and protocol envelopes.

A required workflow is interrupted by settings, internals or background that do not affect the next action.

Reader cost: Readers must decide which details are prerequisites and which can wait.

Pattern example

Before your first review, read about theme choice, display density and protocol envelopes.

Possible revision

Open a file and select a passage. See the reference for display options.

Display options are optional; the bridge is a genuine prerequisite for that live-page workflow.

Can stay as written: Enable the development bridge before reviewing the running page.

Counter-prompt

Review aim: Give detail where it changes the next action

Recognition, exceptions and sources

Optional controls, deep implementation details or tangential background interrupt the shortest complete route to the stated task.

Possible cues: layout settings before first review; authentication protocol before opening the app; unrelated architecture aside.

Exceptions: A prerequisite or material failure mode may be essential before the action; reference pages intentionally contain detail.

Unprioritized laundry listBefore opening a file, consider login, themes, caching, locales, analytics, rollout and keyboard shortcuts.

The response lists every adjacent concern without separating prerequisites, relevant choices and unrelated possibilities.

Reader cost: The reader inherits the work of deciding what matters and where to start.

Pattern example

Before opening a file, consider login, themes, caching, locales, analytics, rollout and keyboard shortcuts.

Possible revision

Open the registered folder and choose a file. Configure a model route if you need generated alternatives.

The folder is already registered; the listed display and operations topics are not prerequisites for opening its file.

Can stay as written: This reference lists every supported configuration key.

Counter-prompt

Review aim: Prioritize the decisions required for this task

Recognition, exceptions and sources

An answer to a bounded task becomes an exhaustive list of possible concerns, with no ordering by relevance or dependency.

Possible cues: consider A, B, C and many unrelated topics; flat list of optional and required work.

Exceptions: A requested exhaustive inventory, compliance checklist or API reference needs complete coverage. Preserve actual dependencies and material risks.

Forced triads and canned Q&AWhy source? Because clarity. Why review? Because confidence. Why Git? Because trust.

Distinct ideas are squeezed into the same three-part slogan or question-and-answer rhythm even when that form adds no meaning.

Reader cost: The repeated performance obscures which facts and relationships actually differ.

Pattern example

Why source? Because clarity. Why review? Because confidence. Why Git? Because trust.

Possible revision

Review decisions retain the source version. Git records changes when you commit the files.

The product behavior is established; the recurring questions are ornamental rather than actual user questions. The separate FAQ answers a real compatibility question.

Can stay as written: Q: Which version is supported? A: Protocol version 1.

Counter-prompt

Review aim: Let the content determine its rhetorical structure

Recognition, exceptions and sources

A fixed rhetorical template determines the number and shape of points instead of the actual ideas, or filler is added to complete a triad.

Possible cues: why X? because Y repeated without an actual reader question; a third point added only to complete a triad.

Exceptions: A real FAQ, a mnemonic with three necessary parts, deliberate approved positioning and parallel API entries can justify a recurring form.

Claims and evidence

Roadmap inside the usage guideThe export will be able to carry Git decisions.

Instructions for the available product drift into features that are only proposed.

Reader cost: Readers cannot reliably tell which instructions work today.

Pattern example

The export will be able to carry Git decisions.

Possible revision

The export contains the selected findings.

Findings export is verified. The Git extension moves to a separate internal plan; no available behavior is removed.

Can stay as written: The job will run after the current upload completes.

Counter-prompt

Review aim: Describe the available behavior

Recognition, exceptions and sources

A usage guide mixes implemented behavior with proposals, temporary rollout history or hypothetical capabilities.

Possible cues: will support; would allow; in a future release.

Exceptions: Roadmaps, migration guides and release notes intentionally discuss time and change; asynchronous behavior may require future tense.

  • Present tense

    Describe general implemented behavior in the present; preserve future tense for actual future timing. Grammar alone cannot determine feature maturity.

  • Future features

    Keep unannounced future capabilities out of ordinary product documentation. Google's internal publication-approval requirement is not adopted as a Voice authorization rule. Explicit plans remain valid document types.

Hedge stackingThe file may generally be considered to remain local.

Several vague qualifiers surround an established fact without identifying an actual unknown or choice.

Reader cost: Readers cannot distinguish genuine uncertainty from habitual caution.

Pattern example

The file may generally be considered to remain local.

Possible revision

The file stays in the registered folder.

Local storage is verified; the separate stale-result condition remains necessary.

Can stay as written: The result may be stale if the source changed outside Studio.

Counter-prompt

Review aim: Keep uncertainty specific and necessary

Recognition, exceptions and sources

Stacked hedges weaken a verified fact, or a suggested simplification would remove an actual condition, unknown or risk.

Possible cues: generally may perhaps; preferred without a stated alternative; repeated generic disclaimers.

Exceptions: Scientific uncertainty, security conditions, configuration dependence, estimates and genuine alternatives require qualification.

Invented cause and benefitGit storage therefore makes every review easier to understand.

A feature is treated as proof of an outcome even though the link is missing from the evidence.

Reader cost: Readers may make a decision based on a benefit that was never established.

Pattern example

Git storage therefore makes every review easier to understand.

Possible revision

Git storage records changes to the review files.

File history is supported; universal comprehension improvement has not been established.

Can stay as written: A stale version is rejected because its hash differs from the current source.

Counter-prompt

Review aim: Show the link between cause and benefit

Recognition, exceptions and sources

A feature is followed by a benefit, conclusion or recommendation that the provided evidence does not establish.

Possible cues: therefore reduces cognitive load; this ensures success; because it is Git-based.

Exceptions: A documented mechanism, bounded measurement or explicitly stated hypothesis can justify a causal explanation.

Claim without evidenceAll integrations are verified.

A guarantee, measurement, readiness claim or evaluative modifier asserts more than the evidence or stated criteria support.

Reader cost: The apparent certainty hides what was and was not checked.

Pattern example

All integrations are verified.

Possible revision

The TypeScript and JavaScript examples passed the consumer checks.

Those checks ran and support exactly that subset; no broader integration claim follows.

Can stay as written: Both examples passed the two recorded checks.

Counter-prompt

Review aim: Match the claim to its evidence

Recognition, exceptions and sources

A count, superlative, guarantee, release claim or evaluative modifier exceeds the observed scope, omits its decision criterion, or treats plausible source text as verified product behavior.

Possible cues: all pages verified; production-ready; guaranteed improvement; percentages without conditions; valid exceptions without a stated criterion.

Exceptions: An exact API invariant or properly scoped measurement can support absolute wording. A stated decision criterion can justify a modifier. For example, valid JSON names a syntactic requirement; it does not mean that a schema was checked. Exception criteria may be supplied by the adjacent rule instead of repeated in every heading.

Unsupported completion claimThe website is live and fully verified.

A report announces completion or readiness without a matching observation for that exact result.

Reader cost: Readers may treat an unverified step as completed and stop checking too early.

Pattern example

The website is live and fully verified.

Possible revision

The local build passed. The public deployment has not been checked.

Only the local build was observed; publication must have its own evidence.

Can stay as written: The deployment URL returned the recorded build identifier.

Counter-prompt

Review aim: Report the observed result and its boundary

Recognition, exceptions and sources

A progress or completion claim lacks a matching tool result, or success in one check is presented as broader readiness.

Possible cues: done without result evidence; tests passed used as editorial approval; source push described as deployment.

Exceptions: A brief result can refer to an accessible report rather than repeat every log line. Verified results do not need speculative hedging.

Unearned praise and automatic agreementBrilliant idea! Your review design is unquestionably the best approach.

The response praises the user or endorses a proposal before examining the claim, evidence or tradeoff.

Reader cost: Approval can replace the useful critique the reader asked for.

Pattern example

Brilliant idea! Your review design is unquestionably the best approach.

Possible revision

The design keeps decisions beside their source. It still needs a rule for stale results.

The reviewed design has source-linked decisions but no stale-result policy; the assessment concerns the design, not the author.

Can stay as written: The new diagram is easier to follow because each arrow names an operation.

Counter-prompt

Review aim: Evaluate the proposal on its merits

Recognition, exceptions and sources

A request for assessment receives flattering approval without a specific reason, or the answer changes its factual position only to agree with the user.

Possible cues: brilliant idea without assessment; you are absolutely right before checking; reflexive praise before critique.

Exceptions: A supported compliment, ordinary warmth and an explicit celebration are valid. Agreement is appropriate when the evidence supports it.

Forced neutrality and false balanceApplying the stale diff and refreshing the source are equally reasonable options.

The answer gives alternatives equal weight even though the supplied facts rule one out or support them unequally.

Reader cost: Readers receive an artificial dilemma instead of the supported distinction.

Pattern example

Applying the stale diff and refreshing the source are equally reasonable options.

Possible revision

Refresh the source before preparing the change; the saved diff refers to an earlier version.

The saved source version is known to be stale; the backend does not support applying that stale proposal.

Can stay as written: Both storage adapters meet the requirements. Choose according to the deployment constraints.

Counter-prompt

Review aim: Weight alternatives by the evidence

Recognition, exceptions and sources

An evidence-based question is answered with interchangeable pros and cons or a generic “both are valid” despite a material difference already established in context.

Possible cues: both options are equally valid despite contrary evidence; it depends without naming the dependency.

Exceptions: Genuine uncertainty, value tradeoffs and comparably supported alternatives require balanced treatment. A request to explain a viewpoint is not an endorsement request.

    Describes evidence-proportionate treatment of viewpoints and constructive critique rather than reflexive agreement. A statement of intended behavior, not a measured account of model compliance or a mandate to choose a side when evidence is missing.

Words and sentence patterns

Mechanism without an operationGit is the preferred source of record.

Abstract labels such as “source of record” replace the file, actor or operation the reader needs to identify.

Reader cost: Readers still do not know what is stored, where it is stored or who changes it.

Pattern example

Git is the preferred source of record.

Possible revision

Commit the approved decisions as JSON files in the repository.

This is an explicit workflow instruction with an established JSON layout, not a claim that Studio commits automatically.

Can stay as written: Git is the preferred option when both supported storage adapters are available.

Counter-prompt

Review aim: Name the object and operation

Recognition, exceptions and sources

An abstract label leaves the reader unable to identify what data exists, where it lives or what operation happens.

Possible cues: source of record; mechanism; ecosystem alignment; orchestration surface.

Exceptions: A defined domain term is useful in a specialist reference; a concept may require explanation before implementation detail.

  • Avoid jargon

    Specialist shorthand can help a specialist audience and obstruct other readers. Does not justify banning technical terms or identifying the author of a sentence.

Invented compound labelsInspect the exact-head evidence-ready review slice.

Several concepts are compressed into a new label that the product never defines.

Reader cost: Readers decode terminology rather than understand the operation.

Pattern example

Inspect the exact-head evidence-ready review slice.

Possible revision

Inspect the review result for this commit.

The compound has no documented meaning beyond the commit's review result.

Can stay as written: The sourceVersion field contains the file hash.

Counter-prompt

Review aim: Use names the reader can resolve

Recognition, exceptions and sources

New compound labels, unexplained abbreviations or synonyms force the reader to infer concepts that already have stable names.

Possible cues: hyphen-stacked labels; multiple names for one field; private working shorthand.

Exceptions: Exact API identifiers, established specialist terms and intentionally defined concepts must remain stable.

Decorative phrases obscure a subject or operation that readers could identify directly.

Pattern example

Possible revision

Model selection and prompt structure

The informational heading introduces a comparison without established results. The cache metaphor is useful when explaining response reuse to this audience.

Narration and working history

Development diaryThe original readiness folder mixed several different things. Only some belong to the product. The maintained structure gives each kind an explicit owner and retention policy.

A public guide retells earlier drafts, cleanup work or feedback rounds instead of describing the current files and behavior.

Reader cost: Readers reconstruct development history to find the current answer.

Pattern example

The original readiness folder mixed several different things. Only some belong to the product. The maintained structure gives each kind an explicit owner and retention policy.

Possible revision

Product code lives in frontend/, backend/ and packages/. Reusable commands live in scripts/. Dated verification reports live in docs/verification/.

The public guide needs the current file locations. The earlier folder cleanup does not affect the reader’s task. The before passage is the user-provided Voice documentation example, not a provider output capture.

Can stay as written: Version 2 renamed the field; existing integrations must migrate.

Counter-prompt

Review aim: Separate the result from working history

Recognition, exceptions and sources

The deliverable recounts abandoned approaches, internal filenames or conversation turns that do not explain the present result.

Possible cues: we initially considered; after your earlier feedback; during this session.

Exceptions: Decision records, incident reports, changelogs and research notebooks need history and provenance.

A heading or introduction adds significance, interpretation or a result promise that the supplied material does not establish.

Reader cost: Readers must separate the actual information from added interpretation.

Pattern example

Model choice: why the smartest route changes everything.

Possible revision

Choose a model for the review.

The section gives model-selection steps; the supplied material does not support a wider outcome claim.

Can stay as written: This reference covers protocol version 1.

Recognition, exceptions and sources

Framing adds an interpretive premise or result promise unsupported by the supplied material, or announces importance without useful scope.
